# Sources and adaptation boundaries

Accessed October 25, 2026. Sources below were actually opened and read. No source illustrations or audio reused.

1. Toan Ngo, The Smart Local Vietnam, September 4, 2021: [9 Ghosts And Demons In Vietnamese Folklore](https://thesmartlocal.com/vietnam/vietnamese-ghosts-demons/), section 1. Supports the bogeyman comparison, parental warnings, child abduction in sacks and the motif of three sacks, nine straps and twelve eyes. This is modern popular cultural reporting, not an original historical manuscript.
2. Phan Chi, Znews, September 23, 2026: [“Ông Ba Bị” làm nghề gì?](https://znews.vn/ong-ba-bi-lam-nghe-gi-post1684590.html). Reports dictionary definitions of an imaginary frightening figure and several different proposed origins. We do not choose any origin hypothesis as established history.

## Narrated claims

- Shots 1–5: folklore description, supported by the above sources. The English warning is a paraphrase, not a verbatim translation attributed to a specific historical speaker.
- Shots 6–12: original nightmare explicitly introduced with “Now imagine…”. The feeding implication, breathing bags, impossible anatomy, living ceiling and room-as-sack ending are invented for this production.
- We did not find firm support in these sources for describing child-eating as a universal defining feature of Ong Ba Bi. It is therefore not presented as a documented cultural fact.
- No assertion that this being exists, that an abduction occurred, that a particular historical person was a demon, or that all Vietnamese people share these beliefs.

Visual assets are newly AI-generated interpretations. No archival or evidentiary status is implied.
